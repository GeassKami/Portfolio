ASME Section VIII Design-by-Analysis — DN50 Float Trap
Forbes Marshall · ASME DBA · Pressure-Retaining Equipment Certification
›
Problem
Non-standard shell, cover, flange, and flat-head geometry of the DN50 float trap could not be reliably assessed using Design-by-Rule equations without major unconservative assumptions. Required formal ASME Sec VIII Div 2 Part 5 Design-by-Analysis.
Methodology
  • Built ANSYS Mechanical model with pressure-retaining components, bolts, contact, and bolt pre-tension
  • Performed mesh sensitivity, global equilibrium, and hand-calculation sanity checks
  • Used stress classification lines and stress linearisation to evaluate Pm, PL, Pb, and Q — not von Mises peak stress alone
  • Evaluated pressure, hydrotest, low-temperature service, and gasket-seating load cases
Outcome
Analysis demonstrated acceptable results for all documented load cases. Supported pressure-retaining design assessment, ASME U2 audit compliance, and multi-jurisdiction certification (ASME/IBR/CSA) across 30+ product variants.

Nonlinear Explicit FEA — SPIF of Ti-6Al-4V (M.Tech Research)
IIT Bhubaneswar · Abaqus/Explicit · Large Deformation · Experimental Validation
›
Problem
Single Point Incremental Forming of Ti-6Al-4V sheet involves severe nonlinear deformation, evolving tool-sheet contact with friction, and a transient prescribed tool trajectory — not representable by conventional linear FEA.
Methodology
  • Selected Abaqus/Explicit; defined elastic-plastic material behaviour, rigid tool motion, frictional surface contact
  • Compared shell vs 3D brick formulations; controlled mass scaling; monitored energy/element behaviour
  • Post-processed forming force, PEEQ, thickness, and contact behaviour
  • Compared results with physical experiments for correlation
Outcome
Reproduced experimental force trends with less than 12% deviation in predicted sheet thickness — providing a validated basis for studying SPIF process behaviour.

CFD → Structural FEA → Fatigue: Rotating Blower Impeller
Forbes Marshall · One-Way FSI · Aerodynamic + Centrifugal Loading · High-Cycle Fatigue
›
Problem
Rotating blower impeller subjected to aerodynamic pressure and centrifugal loading at operating speed, with cyclic loading creating potential high-cycle fatigue risk at blade-root and plate-junction regions.
Methodology
  • Solved fluid problem in ANSYS Fluent and extracted pressure and thermal loading
  • Transferred loads to structural FEA model (ANSYS Mechanical) and combined with centrifugal loading
  • Evaluated stress concentration at blade-root/joint; applied mean/alternating stress for Goodman fatigue assessment
  • Interpreted result critically — not relying only on peak von Mises stress
Outcome
Identified blade-root region as critical. Provided recommendations on root-fillet geometry, operating speed, material/weld considerations, and vibration monitoring based on the coupled FSI-fatigue assessment.
